On December 31st, 2008, thousands of Microsoft Zune music players, the 30-gigabyte model, froze solid at the same time. All of them, all at once, worldwide. No one dropped them. No update went out. They just all woke up on the last day of a leap year, and got stuck in a loop they couldn't escape. By the next morning, they all worked again. This is the Zune leap year bug, and it's the cleanest example of an infinite loop you'll ever see.

Here's what the Zune was doing. Like most devices, it keeps time as a count, in this case the number of days since January 1st, 1980. To show you a date, it has to turn that day count into a year. The way it does this is a simple loop: start at 1980, and keep subtracting a year's worth of days from the count until what's left is less than a full year. Each time you subtract a full year, you add one to the year. When the remaining days are less than a year, you stop, and the leftover is the day within that final year.

The catch is leap years. Most years have 365 days, but a leap year has 366. So the loop has to check: is this year a leap year? If yes, try to subtract 366. If no, subtract 365. 2008 was a leap year.

Now walk through the last day of 2008 with the actual logic. The loop runs year after year, subtracting days, and eventually it gets down to the final year, 2008, with exactly 366 days remaining in the counter. The code checks: is 2008 a leap year? Yes. So it looks at the 366 days left and asks, is this a full leap year or more? It needs to handle the case where exactly 366 days remain, the very last day of the leap year. And in that one case, the code was written so that it neither subtracted the 366 days nor exited the loop. It just went back to the top, saw 366 days still sitting there, checked again, saw a leap year again, and came back to the same stuck spot. Forever. The player wasn't crashed. It was alive, running as fast as it could, around a circle with no exit.

Why did they all recover the next day? Because the bug only existed when the counter held exactly 366, the 366th day of a leap year, December 31st. The instant the real-world clock ticked into January 1st, 2009, the day count became 367, a value for the next year, and the condition that trapped the loop no longer held. The devices that were power-cycled after midnight booted up fine. The trap was a single value, on a single day, that only occurs once every four years.

One leap day. One missing exit. And a music player that counted its way into a corner it couldn't get out of.
